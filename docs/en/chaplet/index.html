The Chaplet of Tears — the text

The prayers of the Coroa das Lágrimas as the devotion's publishers print them today, in Portuguese, with a working translation

Most of the Church acts this chronology has been able to date have a printed text as their object. Bishop Barreto's imprimatur of 8 March 1932, and the four approvals granted abroad in 1935, permit the printing of this chaplet's prayers and of a medal — and judge nothing about what was reported at Campinas. This page shows the text those acts were about.

Reproducing a prayer is not endorsing the claim behind it. No judgment by Church authority on the Campinas apparitions has been located (the chronology page sets out, act by act, what was and was not judged), and nothing here says that these words were dictated to anyone. What the Portuguese below attests is narrower and checkable: it is the wording the devotion's own publishers print today. No copy of the 1932–1935 printings — the ones whose approval the chronology dates — was located, so where today's wording departs from theirs cannot be said.[26] [7] [27]

How the chaplet is arranged

The beads

Forty-nine white beads in seven groups of seven, each group preceded by a larger bead of the same colour, and three further beads to close. The three sources cited here agree on that arrangement, and on counting it as a rosary is counted: the large bead where an Our Father would fall, the seven where the Hail Marys would. The words said on them are the chaplet's own, and are given below.

The medal

The chaplet ends in a medal bearing Our Lady of Tears on one face and Jesus Manietado — Jesus Bound — on the other. Those are the two apparitions the chronology records as reported, in November 1929 and in March and April 1930; the medal is also the object named in the imprimatur of 8 March 1932, alongside the chaplet itself.

Opening prayer

Said at the start, holding the medal

Em nome do Pai, do Filho e do Espírito Santo. Amém.

Eis-nos aqui aos Vossos pés, ó dulcíssimo Jesus Crucificado, para Vos oferecermos as Lágrimas d'Aquela que, com tanto amor, Vos acompanhou no caminho doloroso do Calvário. Fazei, ó bom Mestre, que nós saibamos aproveitar da lição que elas nos dão, para que, na Terra, realizando a Vossa Santíssima Vontade, possamos um dia, no Céu, Vos louvar por toda a eternidade.

Working translation: In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Here we are at your feet, most sweet crucified Jesus, to offer you the tears of her who followed you with such love along the sorrowful road of Calvary. Grant, good Master, that we may profit from the lesson those tears teach us, so that on earth, doing your most holy will, we may one day praise you in heaven for all eternity.

[26] [7]

On each large bead

Once, at the head of each of the seven groups

Vêde, ó Jesus, que são as Lágrimas d'Aquela que mais Vos amou na Terra, e que mais Vos ama no Céu.

Working translation: Behold, O Jesus, these are the tears of her who loved you most on earth, and who loves you most in heaven.

[26] [7] [27]

Publishers differ in spelling rather than in words. Coroa das Lágrimas Oficial prints the older Vêde and capitalises Lágrimas; the Canção Nova sanctuary page prints Vede and lágrimas. Nothing in the sense turns on either, and the reproduction above follows the first.[26] [7]

On each of the seven small beads

Seven times in each group, forty-nine times in all

Meu Jesus, ouvi os nossos rogos, pelas Lágrimas de Vossa Mãe Santíssima.

Working translation: My Jesus, hear our prayers, through the tears of your most holy Mother.

The three final beads

On the three beads that close the chaplet, the invocation of the large beads is said again, three times over. No new text belongs here.[26] [7]

What this rests on: [26] [7]

Final prayer

Virgem Santíssima e Mãe das Dores, nós Vos pedimos que junteis os Vossos rogos aos nossos, a fim de que Jesus, Vosso Divino Filho, a quem nos dirigimos em nome das Vossas Lágrimas de Mãe, ouça as nossas preces e nos conceda, com as graças que desejamos, a coroa eterna. Amém!

Working translation: Most holy Virgin and Mother of Sorrows, we ask you to join your prayers to ours, so that Jesus your divine Son, to whom we turn in the name of your tears as a mother, may hear our petitions and grant us, together with the graces we desire, the eternal crown. Amen.

[26] [7]

The last clause is printed two ways by the two sources cited here. Coroa das Lágrimas Oficial ends a coroa eterna, the eternal crown, and that is the reading reproduced above; the Canção Nova sanctuary page ends a coroa da vida eterna, the crown of eternal life. Neither carries an edition or a date, so neither can be shown to be the older, and the variant is recorded rather than resolved — as the dating disagreements in the chronology are.[26] [7]

Closing invocations

Said at the end, contemplating the medal

Por Vossa mansidão divina, ó Jesus Manietado, salvai o mundo do erro que o ameaça!

Ó Virgem Dolorosíssima, as Vossas Lágrimas derrubaram o império infernal!

Working translation: By your divine meekness, O Jesus Bound, save the world from the error that threatens it!

O most sorrowful Virgin, your tears brought down the infernal empire!

The seven sorrows, and where they come into this

The chaplet as its publishers transmit it prescribes no mysteries. Leaflets that pair one of the seven sorrows of Mary with each of the seven groups are making an arrangement, and it is worth knowing which part is which.

What the sources say about meditation

They say it in general terms. The Portuguese Wikipedia article states that the chaplet is prayed at any hour of the day, sempre meditando na Paixão de Jesus, nas Dores e nas Lágrimas da Virgem Maria — always meditating on the Passion of Jesus, on the Sorrows and on the Tears of the Virgin Mary — and assigns nothing to any particular group. Canção Nova says the structure invites meditation on the Passion of Christ. The novena page cited here, which does set the chaplet inside a nine-day form, likewise assigns no meditation to a group.

Two devotions, kept apart

The same encyclopedia article describes this chaplet as similar to the Coroa das Dores de Maria, the Crown of the Sorrows of Mary, though of a different colour. Similar is not the same: the seven sorrows are an older Marian devotion with its own beads, and the seven groups of this one are not a count of them.

The traditional seven

In their usual order: the prophecy of Simeon; the flight into Egypt; the loss of the child Jesus in the temple; the meeting on the road to Calvary; the crucifixion and death; receiving the body taken down from the cross; the burial. A nine-day arrangement built on that list is set out on the other page of this site, and it is this site's arrangement, not the devotion's.

What this rests on: The list of the seven sorrows is the traditional one, given here so that a reader meeting it in a leaflet can see what is being added to the chaplet and by whom. The pairing of one sorrow per group is not made by any source cited on this page. [27] [7] [28]
