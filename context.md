## The subject in one paragraph

Amália Aguirre (Riós, Ourense, Galicia, Spain, 1901 – Taubaté, Brazil, 1977),
in religion Irmã Amália de Jesus Flagelado, was a member of the first group
of the Instituto das Missionárias de Jesus Crucificado, founded in Campinas,
São Paulo, in 1928 by Bishop Francisco de Campos Barreto and Maria Villac
(Madre Maria do Calvário). From 1928 she was reported to bear stigmata — a
sensation in the Brazilian press, immediately contested by physicians — and
from 1929–1930 to receive apparitions of Jesus ("Jesus Manietado", 8 November
1929) and of Mary presenting herself as "Nossa Senhora das Lágrimas" (8 March
and 8 April 1930), teaching the Chaplet (Coroa/Terço) of Tears and asking for
a medal. The chaplet became a widely practiced Brazilian devotion. In 2023
the Archdiocese of Campinas created a study commission on her possible "odor
of sanctity" toward the possible opening of a beatification cause.

**Scope.** Per core#71 the chronology no longer stops at the edges of the
reported apparitions. It carries what came BEFORE (the erection of the diocese
in 1908, Barreto's installation in 1920, Maria Villac's association of 1917 and
her 1927 assent, Amália's emigration and clothing) and what came AFTER (the
dated imprimaturs of 1932–1935, the withdrawal of the devotion after Barreto's
death, the 1952 Decretum Laudis, the congregation's national standing, Amália's
1953 move to Taubaté and 1977 death, Villac's death, and the 2023 commission
and republication of the writings). Reported miracles follow the core#71 rule
absolutely: the only datable thing is that a cure was REPORTED, by whom and
when first attested, or a recognition act — and there is exactly one such event
here, the 1929 publication of the founding cure narrative in the Institute's own
periodical, recorded as a publication and not as a cure. No cure carries an
approval-ladder rung.

## The source landscape (read before adding references)

- **Primary:** the Archdiocese of Campinas decree of 16 June 2023 (scanned
  PDF on the archdiocesan site; Livro XXXII dos Atos de Governo, Prot. 106).
  The ONLY primary Church document located during the bootstrap. Its recitals
  also attest her birth data, the 1928 founding, and the 1952 Decretum Laudis
  of Pius XII for the congregation.
- **Primary-adjacent:** the congregation's official history at
  mjc.org.br/nossa-historia — which, as consulted on 2026-08-05, recounts the
  founding WITHOUT mentioning Amália or the apparitions. That silence is
  itself a documented datum (see disambiguation).
- **Academic:** Centro de Memória da Unicamp, "O caso Amália de Jesus
  Flagelado: contexto e narrativa" — the 1928–29 press controversy, medical
  skepticism (Dr. Sousa Ribeiro's accusation against Barreto), and the 1929
  defense "Res Non Verba" by Arthur de Vasconcelos. CMU holds archival
  material on the case that has NOT been mined yet.
- **Academic, and evidentially the strongest thing here after the 2023
  decree:** Magno F. de J. Santos, "No silêncio da clausura: videntes de
  aparições marianas no Brasil (1928-1937)", *Rev. Diálogo Educacional* 19:63
  (2019) 1397–1417 — the same historian as the RBHR 2025 article, comparing
  Campinas with Cimbres. It is the ONLY source located that dates the episcopal
  act on the devotion (imprimatur of **8 March 1932**, not the devotional
  1931) and that names the 1935 foreign approvals one by one with granting
  authority, see and — for Sopron — a protocol number. It also supplies the
  1928-05-11 clothing, the 1953 Taubaté transfer, and the post-1941 withdrawal
  of the devotion from public view. It reproduces none of those acts.
- **Devotional video (three items, ONE speaker):** Raphael Tonon, in a Centro
  Dom Bosco lecture and two podcasts (2020s, ~61k words transcribed, vaulted in
  the cronologia archive). Treat as one position stated three times. Two things
  make them worth more than ordinary devotional media and two make them worth
  less. Worth more: the 2023 decree names Tonon a cooperator of the study
  commission, so he has had access others have not, and he names archival
  material out loud (the Livro do Tombo; "A Missionária"). Worth less: he
  produces no document, and he contradicts himself and the record on datable
  points (founding 25 Dec 1927 in one video and 25 Dec 1928 in another; Villac
  dead in 1978 against the congregation's 1984; Zeller made bishop in 1945
  against the documented 1939, and called abbot of Rio, which he never was).
- **Devotional (derivative):** Canção Nova, Coroa das Lágrimas Oficial,
  Milícia da Imaculada, many blogs. They transmit the apparition narrative,
  the words attributed to Mary and Jesus, the 8-Nov-1929 / 8-Mar-1930 /
  8-Apr-1930 dates, and the 1931/1934/1935 approval claims — and cite no
  documents. They recycle one another; treat agreement among them as ONE
  attestation, not several.
- **Tertiary:** pt/en Wikipedia follow the devotional literature.

## Open questions for deep investigation

1. Locate the episcopal act on the chaplet. The date to request is now most
   likely **8 March 1932** (Santos 2019), not the devotional 8 March 1931, and
   the series to request is the **Livro do Tombo / acts of government** in the
   archdiocesan archive at the Cúria Metropolitana de Campinas, which a
   commission cooperator states he has read. This is the single highest-value
   request this project can make.
2. The reported 20 Feb 1934 declaration is now doubly doubtful: neither Santos
   article records it, and the one video that gives a day gives 20 February
   *1932*. Ask whether any 1934 act exists at all, or whether the date is a
   corruption of the 1932 imprimatur.
3. The foreign imprimaturs are now addressable individually rather than as a
   rumour — Detroit (Gallagher, 22 Mar 1935), Munich and Freising (Buchwieser,
   22 Mar 1935), Sopron (**nihil obstat nº 924/1935**, 25 May 1935), Győr
   (Breyer, 13 Jul 1935). Santos's footnote also lists Saint Paul–Minneapolis
   under Archbishop **John Robert Roach**, which cannot be 1935 (he held the see
   1975–1995); worth writing to Santos about.
4. **Answered this pass:** Villac's death (1 Mar 1984, contradicted by Tonon's
   1978 — still worth a register), Amália's burial (Franciscan cemetery beside
   the Igreja de Santa Clara, Taubaté), and when she moved to Taubaté (1953,
   Santos 2019). Still open: *why* she moved.
5. Read the **"Virgo Lacrimarum" note** the archdiocese published alongside the
   16 June 2023 decree. Its existence is established by the archdiocese's own
   headline; the page 404s after a site migration and Wayback was rate-limited.
   Request it from the chancery by name, and ask for its protocol number.
6. Whether the current Archdiocese has said anything about the apparitions
   as such (the 2023 decree does not), and how the annual celebrations at the
   Basílica do Carmo relate to official status (local press coverage exists
   but was bot-blocked during the bootstrap — acidadeon.com returned 403).
7. Mine the CMU archival collection references and the 1928–1930 Campinas
   press (**Correio Popular**, quoted by Santos 2019 for 1929), and locate a
   holding of **"A Missionária"**, the Institute's own periodical, which is
   where the earliest published narrative of the case would be.
8. Test the video-only Roman story: any trace of a 1935 apostolic visitation of
   Campinas, of Dom Lourenço Zeller OSB in that role, or of a Holy Office
   decree on the chaplet — Vatican Apostolic Archive, the nunciature files,
   AAS of the 1930s. Absence there would be a publishable finding in itself.

## Glossary and cross-links

The shared glossary (cronologia.github.io/glossary) currently has no entries
for imprimatur, beatification, private revelation or Decretum Laudis, so this
dataset uses no `[[term-id]]` links yet. If those terms land in the glossary,
sync `data/glossary-terms.json` and link them from the relevant prose.
